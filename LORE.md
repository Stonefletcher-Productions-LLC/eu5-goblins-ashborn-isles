# Goblins of the Ashborn Isles

In the early years of the fourteenth century, fire rose from the Atlantic. Months of eruptions left behind black volcanic islands. When the smoke cleared, goblins already walked their shores. No one saw them arrive. Some captains claim the mountains birthed them; others speak of passages beneath the earth that have since collapsed. Fishing camps became villages, crude mines opened in the ridges, and rival crews fought over sheltered harbors. By 1337, Hooktooth has become Cindermaw's capital, but the Ashborn Isles remain divided. The Brineward of Brackmaw and the Reefhook, Shatterfin and Sootwake clans share the Emberblood's faith in the Hunger Below, yet follow their own crowns; Shatterfin alone keeps the maternal house of the Tidemothers. Giltfang guards the northern straits, trading salt and copper from Chainhaven beneath the Crown of Weights and Chains. Poor treasuries, crowded settlements and growing fleets drive them toward expansion. Cindermaw holds Lantern Cay between the northern and southern crowns. Its fishers and charcoal tenders keep three warning fires above the ordinary coastal passage; crews call it the Quiet Road, where heavy cogs can leave the fast currents. Every crown dreams of uniting the islands, and every crown means to be the one that does it. Yet let an outsider land on Ashborn soil, and the feuding crowns answer as one Horde. Beyond the Isles lies a world the goblins have only begun to discover.

## The Ashen Covenant

The Ashborn call their shared faith the Ashen Covenant. Beneath the islands sleeps
the Hunger Below, whose fire makes land and whose appetite may consume it. Some
say the first goblins were born from that fire; others remember passages that
closed behind them. No common account settles the mystery.

"What keeps you living must be fed." Fire, water, roots and the remembered dead
lend their gifts, and every gift creates an obligation. A chief owes protection
and provisions to the households that supply his crews. A rescued sailor owes
labor to the beacon keepers. A woodcutter owes care to the grove that shelters the
next generation. Offerings are useful things: food, rope, charcoal and finished
craftwork, with animal sacrifice at some feasts.

The Returning Mother receives the household cords of Shatterfin. Brackmaw's Reed
Listener witnesses agreements beside water. Reefhook tends lamps for the Lantern
Dead, including strangers whose names are lost. Sootwake's Rootkeeper shelters
burial trees and seed groves. Chainhaven's merchants swear their measures before
the Silent Scale, which weighs every debt. At the Storm Teeth, crews honor the
Tooth in the Gale. These powers belong to the shared faith; migrating households carry their
observances between islands rather than changing religion at a border.

Oathkeepers preserve names, witness agreements, heal and interpret omens. They
may be women or men; inheritance, apprenticeship and public recognition vary by
shrine. Their authority can restrain a chief, but gifts and family loyalties can
also purchase a convenient interpretation. The island crowns have no common
religious head at the beginning of the campaign.

The sacred places are young: fissures, pools, cairns, ropes and living groves on
recently emerged islands. Carried relics and remembered chants hint at an older
past without proving where the Ashborn came from. The covenant can welcome an
outsider adopted into a household, yet its promise of reciprocal duties also
raises difficult questions about those held in slavery.


## Cindermaw — Emberblood

King Drogg Cindermaw, the Stone Fletcher, earned his name defending the Hooktooth approaches when his crews ran short of iron arrowheads. He put stonecutters to work shaping volcanic points, supplied the ridge archers and held the passes until his scattered captains could gather. He still keeps one of those crude arrows beside his throne. Broad in ambition and exacting in preparation, Drogg inspects weapons, remembers which captains held their ground and rewards useful service with a place near his table. His patience ends with commanders who waste lives for display. He treats the independence of the other crowns as a problem that strength and obligation will eventually settle.

House Cindermaw built its standing by binding rival forge crews and ridge captains to a common muster. Its saying is 'One fire, many blades.' The royal household eats beneath captured shields, but every claimant to its favor must name the service that earned a seat. Queen Grakka of House Ashjaw insists that stores and wages be counted before another expedition is promised. Their adult son Grask seeks a command worthy of his father's reputation; daughter Kragga follows the petitions and marriages through which the court extends its reach. The dynasty presents itself as the house capable of speaking for all Ashborn. Its captains agree readily on Cindermaw's greatness and less readily on who should profit from it.

Cindermaw is the largest Ashborn kingdom, anchored by Hooktooth's crowded harbor and the forges beneath its volcanic ridges. Its population and workshops give Drogg the deepest foundation for sustained war among the island crowns. Military service shapes status, and successful captains become patrons whose retainers fill both the muster yards and the royal hall. Hooktooth's influence travels through weapons contracts, marriage negotiations, paid aid and promises of protection. Smaller houses seek royal favor even when their own rulers resist Drogg's claims. His court increasingly treats an assembly of equal crowns as the first step toward a single Ashborn command. Lantern Cay guards the Quiet Road between Cindermaw and Giltfang. Lantern Haven lands fish for passing crews, Wickwood tends the beacon fuel, and Emberwatch keeps three fires above the passage. Drogg claims the cay through the captains who first supplied those lights; its households owe watch duty to Hooktooth.

Drogg wants the Gathering to acknowledge Cindermaw's leadership, then turn the islands' combined strength toward the eastern horizon. Brackmaw can obstruct him through provisions and coastal industry; Reefhook's pilots and Sootwake's timber remain valuable to any fleet he assembles. Shatterfin's Tidemother demands recognition of her own house and succession. Influence gives Drogg opportunities to bind these powers through bargains, but his expectation of obedience can turn useful partners into a coalition against him. At home, long campaigns consume the grain, wages and labor that keep Hooktooth loyal. Cindermaw has the strength to lead the Isles; Drogg must make its leadership worth accepting, or pay the price of imposing it.

## Brackmaw — Brineward

Murgash Brackmaw, the Sluice-King, earned his name during the Blackwater Flood. He ordered his own hall's embankment cut to spare Brackhaven's granaries, then bound the feuding marsh houses to rebuild the tidal gates together. Patient, exacting and fiercely proud of his house, he remembers every debt and inspects the sluices himself. His enemies say he prices friendship by the barrel. Murgash answers that hungry allies cannot hold a causeway.

Brackmaw's strength runs through its waterways. Raised roads join the reedlands to Brackhaven, where timber, rope and preserved fish provision the coast. The marsh houses guard their own gates and demand a voice whenever the crown calls for labor. Murgash seeks to make Brackhaven the indispensable harbor of the Ashborn Isles: every pact supplied from its storehouses, every fleet dependent on its workshops. Cindermaw's appetite for timber and provisions offers both a market and a point of pressure. Yet a neglected embankment or a house denied its due can close the channels on which his authority rests.

## Reefhook — Reefstrider

Skrezz Reefhook, the Wreck-Taker, won his name at the Lantern Shoals. With the harbor boats scattered by a squall, he led a relief crew through a submerged channel and brought home the stranded fishers, then stripped their abandoned hulls before the next tide. He made the salvaged timber into boats for households that had lost their catch. Quick with a joke and quicker to spot a bargaining weakness, Skrezz cultivates visiting captains personally. He spends tomorrow's pearl revenue freely and leaves his court to settle the accounts.

House Reefhook traces its authority to Zrekk's first chain of rescue beacons, maintained by each harbor household in return for pilotage rights. Its saying is 'Bring the crew home; reckon the cargo after.' Skrezz wears the house's split-shell clasp rather than a jeweled crown. His older brother Krizzek is the adult dynastic successor under the existing strength law; in court he presses the claims of fishing households against Skrezz's costly foreign friendships. Consort Zikka of House Shoalcut brings ties to the pilot families. Their young children Zikrat and Skrizza make the future of the rescue line a matter of public concern.

Reefhook's growing settlements need safe fishing grounds, dependable buyers and grain from larger islands. Pearl wealth arrives in bursts, while nets and boats need repair every season. Skrezz wants the other crowns to treat Reefhook's pilots as indispensable partners. Harbor elders fear that selling their soundings will surrender the very advantage that keeps the island independent. Divers demand a fair share of rich finds; widowed households expect the house to honor its rescue obligations even in a lean year. Reefhook can turn navigation and trade into influence, but every lost boat tests the crown's claim to rule.

## Sootwake — Ashveil

Snikh Sootwake, the Blackbough, earned his name in the Red Hollow fire. When smoke cut off the ridge settlements, he held a narrow firebreak while families escaped along the charcoal tracks. He carried a scorched branch into his father's hall and later kept it above his seat as a reminder of the crown's debt to those families. Spare with words and suspicious of visiting envoys, he walks boundary lines with the wardens and knows which paths can carry an armed company. He is patient with saplings and harsh with anyone who breaks a cutting oath.

House Sootwake descends from Sizh's band of hearth keepers, who joined scattered clearings through guarded paths and common firebreaks. Its saying is 'Roots outlast fire.' The family seats its children at an unpainted rootwood table, where each generation cuts a new mark beside the old ones. Snikh's older brother Zhor is the adult dynastic successor under the existing strength law and argues that the house owes its wardens generous timber rights. Consort Zheska of House Cinderhush speaks for the charcoal settlements that need the same trees. Their children Khash and Khazra inherit a house whose unity depends on balancing those claims.

Sootwake's expanding settlements give the crown more hands, hearths and mouths to feed. Timber and charcoal can buy independence, yet selling too much leaves bare hills and exposed roads. Snikh wants enough cleared land to feed his people while preserving a forest through which invaders must follow paths his wardens know. Brackmaw's workshops and Cindermaw's forges both want Ashveil wood; their contracts can enrich the kingdom or divide its court. Woodland households defend their cutting rights, and the crown cannot demand endless labor without losing the trust that keeps its scattered settlements together.

## Giltfang - Cinderweight

Vrekk Giltfang, the Brass-Tooth, lost part of his jaw when a rival challenged his copper weights. He returned with a brass tooth and bought the debts of the rival household. Their warehouse became his palace. He intends to make the other crowns depend on a harbor they cannot command.

House Giltfang first settled quarrels over wreckage hauled onto steaming beaches. Its scales became accepted throughout the northern harbors, and the weighing fee paid for armed crews. The house keeps both the weights and the crown.

Chainhaven lies inside a sheltered volcanic bay on Giltfang. Copper workings and salt pans supply its market, while the smaller island of Tolltooth watches the northern approaches. Limited grain and timber leave the merchant houses dependent on cargo from the older islands. Their first weighed offering descends into a sea fissure below the counting hall.

Hooktooth controls the old trade. Vrekk wants Chainhaven to become the northern exchange, buying provisions from Brackmaw and timber from Sootwake while hiring Reefhook pilots and bargaining with Shatterfin captains. He measures success in ships that cannot sail without his credit. Across the strait, the Stormfang of Shatter Maw outnumber his crews and owe his houses money. Vrekk considers their debts a guarantee of peace. His captains, watching Shatterfin's hulls multiply, are less certain.

## Shatterfin — Stormfang

Jaimzha Shatterfin, the Mare-Mother, rules the Stormfang settlements across three north-western islands, Shatter Maw. Captains bring her disputes over crews and passage, but she judges a voyage by the households it must sustain. Her crown rests on the maternal house: senior women carry its memory, settle its obligations and keep its succession visible to every harbor. Jaimzha approaches the Gathering as a ruler with terms of her own, willing to lead the Isles or bargain for protection without surrendering her house's continuity.

House Shatterfin passes its crown through maternal seniority. Jaimzha's younger sister Skritcha is the initial eligible successor, ahead of Jaimzha's daughters Morzha and Rikkra. Her son Vrosh may serve the realm, but cannot inherit under the Tidemother law. The family's saying is 'The tide returns to its mother.' Its women disagree over how much security a stronger crown can offer, yet share an interest in keeping the house and its obligations intact.

Shatterfin's Stormfang people cross the water between Shatterfin, Knifeback and Maw Rock for work, kinship and defense. Crews depend on safe passages and households ashore depend on their return. Jaimzha's authority joins those shores through the maternal house. Cindermaw offers military weight, Brackmaw provisions and Reefhook skilled pilots; each bargain also gives another crown a claim on Stormfang service. Shatterfin seeks a place in the Gathering that recognizes both its maritime contribution and its distinct succession customs.

Every night the Stormfang crews can see the lamps of Tolltooth across the strait, and every morning they pay Giltfang's tolls to fish their own sea. Shatter Maw has more hands, more hulls and more hungry households than Chainhaven; Chainhaven has the scales, the silver and half of Shatterfin's debts. Jaimzha's captains say the northern straits should answer to the tide, not to the counting hall. Jaimzha herself would rather bind Giltfang than burn it, but she has not told her captains no.
